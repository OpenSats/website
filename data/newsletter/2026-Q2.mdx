Dear Freedom Lovers,

We must build the world we wish to see.

This was the simple premise, the kernel of truth, our north star, that led us
to will OpenSats into existence. An efficient, transparent, sustainable, and
globally scalable nonprofit focused on accelerating freedom technology.
Easier said than done.

The truth is that we were wildly naive on how difficult the task of building
out this mission would be. And yet, here we are, distributing nearly a million
dollars worth of bitcoin to hundreds of grant recipients in over forty
countries every month. Our success is tangible but [the mission](/mission)
remains a challenge, there is plenty of work left to be done.

We could not have accomplished what we have without your support. Thousands of
people have donated. Thousands of builders have applied for grants. The work of
our mostly volunteer team combining the two. We are focused on accelerating
technology, but it is people, motivated individuals, that push this movement
forward.

It should not be a surprise that our pain point is also people. There remains a
shortage of qualified, responsible, trustworthy volunteers. Working on it. We
have also had difficulty securing [recurring financial support](/monthly) from both
individuals and companies, many of which benefit from the open source software
we support. Also working on it, but this aspect feels even more uphill. Raising
money is hard. Feels like a treadmill. This is why a large base of recurring
support is so important for our sustainable future. On the bright side, we hold
all donations in bitcoin multisig, which provides us both sovereignty and
purchasing power benefits.

Life is short, the grind is long, the mission is more important than ever. If
you are reading this, do more, whether that is
[supporting our work at OpenSats](/donate) or doing your own thing in parallel
to accelerate freedom tech. We need it. Our
children need it. The future is bright if we make it bright.

If not us, then who? If not now, then when?

Push forward.  
Never stop.

## Building the Base

Developing software is hard. Even with all the agentic tools that today's
LLM-driven world has to offer, creating and maintaining reliable software is a
difficult and strenuous task.

Any long-lasting endeavor requires a solid foundation, and Bitcoin is no
exception. OpenSats' mission is to help provide funding so that said foundation
can be built. From [open hardware](/blog/open-hardware-for-open-money) to
[nostr libraries](/blog/the-libraries-behind-open-communication) for open and
censorship-resistant communication, to
[base-layer privacy](/blog/call-for-applications-spring-2026) for bitcoin
itself: OpenSats funds it all.

Whether bull or bear market, we are here to support the builders. Those who are
working on making bitcoin and the internet more open, more resilient, and more
sovereign for those who dare to empower themselves. People like
[Calvin Kim](/blog/calvin-kim-receives-lts-grant), who is working on client
diversity and [Utreexo](/projects/utreexod), and recently joined our
[long-term support roster](/tags/lts) to continue this work.

The base of any project—any human endeavor—is people. Whether it's software or
hardware, buildings or protocols. It is people that bring these things into
existence, and it is people that improve and maintain them.

## Software Shipped

Our grantees shipped over 500 releases this quarter, spread across more than
90 of the [hundreds of projects](/heartbeat) we support. Some highlights:

- **Cluster mempool shipped:**
  [Bitcoin Core 31.0](https://github.com/bitcoin/bitcoin/releases/tag/v31.0)
  came out in April. Its headline change is a redesign of the mempool
  that orders transactions by the feerate at which they would actually
  be mined. Block building and eviction get smarter, and the new
  replace-by-fee rules close the known ways a replacement could leave
  the mempool worse off. Several of our
  [Bitcoin Core](/projects/bitcoin-core) grantees contributed to the
  release.
- **Splicing you can type:** Core Lightning
  [v26.04](https://github.com/ElementsProject/lightning/releases/tag/v26.04)
  added `splicein` and `spliceout` commands. Last quarter we wrote about
  LTS grantee [Dusty Daemon](/blog/dusty-daemon-receives-lts-grant)
  getting [splicing](/projects/splicing) into the BOLTs. This quarter,
  node operators can resize a channel with a single command. The same
  release made payments more reliable through parallel pathfinding in
  askrene.
- **[Cashu](/projects/cashu) mints learn on-chain:**
  [CDK v0.17.0](https://github.com/cashubtc/cdk/releases/tag/v0.17.0)
  implements NUT-30, which adds on-chain deposits and withdrawals to
  mints that previously spoke Lightning only.
- **Two Utreexo nodes, two releases:**
  [Floresta](/projects/floresta)
  [v0.9.0](https://github.com/vinteumorg/floresta/releases/tag/v0.9.0)
  implements the BIP-183 messaging flow for proof-driven sync, and
  [utreexod](/projects/utreexod)
  [v0.6.0](https://github.com/utreexo/utreexod/releases/tag/v0.6.0)
  sped up historical proof generation. LTS grantee
  [Calvin Kim](/blog/calvin-kim-receives-lts-grant) leads the utreexod
  work.
- **[Krux](/projects/krux) patches what the robots found:**
  [v26.04.0](https://github.com/selfcustody/krux/releases/tag/v26.04.0)
  fixes a batch of security issues that AI-assisted audits surfaced in
  the hardware signer, including stricter PSBT checks and protections
  against malicious QR codes and SD cards.
- **New firmware for the [Bitaxe](/projects/bitaxe):**
  [ESP-Miner v2.14.0](https://github.com/bitaxeorg/ESP-Miner/releases/tag/v2.14.0)
  gives the open-source home miner a customizable dashboard, a
  scoreboard, and logs that survive reboots.

Releases are one part of the story. Our grantees also write protocol
specifications, hunt for bugs, and build prototypes of what might come
next:

- The LNHANCE Expedition's
  [MCCV](https://github.com/LNHANCE-Expedition/mccv) vault ("More
  Complicated CTV Vault") can now be tried on signet. This quarter
  brought a working CLI, persistent storage, and a user guide.
- [Smite](https://github.com/morehouse/smite), the Lightning snapshot
  fuzzer we covered last issue, gained an IR-based fuzzing engine and
  `smitebot`, a tool that automates long fuzzing campaigns.
- [Bitcoinfuzz](/projects/bitcoinfuzz) added miniscript and descriptor
  modules and collected fresh trophies, among them a BIP32 seed length
  validation bug that affected several libraries.

As always, [OpenSats heartbeat](/heartbeat) has the full picture: a live
feed of every release, commit, and pull request across the projects we
fund.

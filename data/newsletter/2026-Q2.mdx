## Building the Base

Developing software is hard. Even with all the agentic tools that today's
LLM-driven world has to offer, creating and maintaining reliable software is a
difficult and strenuous task.

Any long-lasting endeavor requires a solid foundation, and Bitcoin is no
exception. OpenSats' mission is to help provide funding so that foundation
can be built. From [open hardware](/blog/open-hardware-for-open-money) to
[nostr libraries](/blog/the-libraries-behind-open-communication) for open and
censorship-resistant communication, to
[base-layer privacy](/blog/call-for-applications-spring-2026) for bitcoin
itself: OpenSats funds it all.

Whether bull or bear market, we are here to support the builders. Those who are
working on making bitcoin and the internet more open, more resilient, and more
sovereign for those who dare to empower themselves. People like
[Calvin Kim](/blog/calvin-kim-receives-lts-grant), who is working on client
diversity and [Utreexo](/projects/utreexod), and recently joined our
[long-term support roster](/tags/lts) to continue this work.

The base of any project—any human endeavor—is people. Whether it's software or
hardware, buildings or protocols. It is people who bring these things into
existence, and it is people who improve and maintain them.

OpenSats' [spotlight](/spotlight) is a series that shines a light on these people. What
drives them, what makes them tick, and how come they ended up working on
freedom tech in the first place?

## Sats Sent

In Q2 2026, OpenSats has sent almost 36 BTC to over 300 recipients all over
the world. The transaction fees for these payouts were 19,435 sats, or \$12.51
at current prices. Given that we've sent over \$2.3 million this quarter we
have paid the equivalent of 0.00054% in transaction fees.

As mentioned in our [last quarterly update](/newsletter/2026-Q1), we have been
sending roughly **\$1,000,000 worth of sats** to our grantees each month. Some
quarters it's a little more, some quarters it's a little less.

Have a look at our [transparency page](/transparency) to dig deeper into our
financial reports or browse our [project pages](/projects) to see how many
sats they've received in total.

We award grants on a rolling basis, meaning that our numbers will fluctuate as
project-specific grants end and new grants are given. In addition to that we
run on a bitcoin standard, which means that the numbers of satoshis we have to
send will fluctuate as well.

In Q2, June was our strongest month in terms of payouts, with 1,285,963,752
sats sent, followed by April and May with 1,207,213,206 and 1,086,310,722 sats
respectively.

Bitcoin allows us to send money almost instantly, without intermediaries,
without single points of failure, while being incredibly convenient and
cost-efficient. It's a testament to how far we've come.

Sign, broadcast, done.

## Software Shipped

Our grantees shipped [over 500 releases](https://heartbeat.opensats.org/?types=release) this quarter, made possible by the funding provided from our [general](/funds/general) and [nostr](/funds/nostr) funds.

Some highlights:

- **Splicing:** Core Lightning
  [v26.04](https://github.com/ElementsProject/lightning/releases/tag/v26.04)
  added `splicein` and `spliceout` commands. Last quarter we wrote about
  LTS grantee [Dusty Daemon](/blog/dusty-daemon-receives-lts-grant)
  getting [splicing](/projects/splicing) into the Lightning specifications (BOLTs). This quarter,
  node operators can resize a channel with a single command. The same
  release made payments more reliable through parallel pathfinding in
  [askrene](https://blog.blockstream.com/core-lightning-v24-08-steel-backed-up-channels/).
- **[Cashu](/projects/cashu) goes on-chain:**
  [CDK v0.17.0](https://github.com/cashubtc/cdk/releases/tag/v0.17.0)
  implements NUT-30, which adds on-chain deposits and withdrawals to
  mints that previously spoke Lightning only.
- **[Floresta](/projects/floresta) and [utreexod](/projects/utreexod):**
  Floresta
  [v0.9.0](https://github.com/vinteumorg/floresta/releases/tag/v0.9.0)
  implements the BIP-183 messaging flow for proof-driven sync, and
  utreexod
  [v0.6.0](https://github.com/utreexo/utreexod/releases/tag/v0.6.0)
  sped up historical proof generation. LTS grantee
  [Calvin Kim](/blog/calvin-kim-receives-lts-grant) leads the utreexod
  work.
- **[Krux](/projects/krux) patches what the robots found:**
  [v26.04.0](https://github.com/selfcustody/krux/releases/tag/v26.04.0)
  fixes a batch of security issues that AI-assisted audits surfaced in
  the hardware signer, including stricter [PSBT](/topics/psbt) checks and protections
  against malicious QR codes and SD cards.

There are too many releases to list, and they only tell one part of the story.
Our grantees also write protocol specifications, do boring maintenance work,
hunt for bugs, and build experimental prototypes of what might come
next.

Some highlights:

## From Foundation to Trending

Two of the foundational areas we support are cryptographic identities and permissionless communication protocols. [Nostr](/funds/nostr) provides solid fundamentals for both of these, allowing seamless communication and collaboration without intermediaries. The foundational work done by [fiatjaf](/blog/fiatjaf-receives-lts-grant) and [our other nostr grantees](/tags/nostr/grants) is often strenuous and mostly invisible. Protocol-level work, relay implementations, and boring cryptographic primitives.

It is all the more exciting, therefore, that this week, two of GitHub's Top 3
trending projects were built on foundations OpenSats funds:
[buzz](https://github.com/block/buzz) and
[bitchat](https://github.com/permissionlesstech/bitchat).

## A New Territory of Freedom

Permissionless protocols and the freedom tech that's built upon them open up a new territory. A territory for people to freely assemble, transact, communicate, and exchange ideas.

[Satoshi Nakamoto](https://satoshi.nakamotoinstitute.org/quotes/motives/) was well aware of this when he started working on Bitcoin. He was also aware that authoritarian entities, those who love to control and silence others, will not be too happy about permissionless freedom tech. "Yes," he said in agreement, conceding the point that not all political problems can be addressed through cryptographic means, "but we can win a major battle in the arms race and gain a new territory of freedom for several years."

As permissionless technology proliferates, this new territory of freedom will continue to be attacked and subverted, by legal and other means. The most recent example is a takedown request by the Indian government sent to our LTS grantee Calle, regarding the aforementioned bitchat app.

## A New Home of Mind

As we're heading further into the agentic age, the age of machine intelligence, I can't help but think of the opening line of John Perry Barlow's [*A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace*](https://www.eff.org/cyberspace-independence), written in 1996:

> Governments of the Industrial World, you weary giants of flesh and steel, I come from Cyberspace, the new home of Mind.

Barlow wrote that "cyberspace consists of transactions, relationships, and thought itself," a construct that is created and made possible by the very act of communication between peers. He describes "a world that all may enter without privilege or prejudice accorded by race, economic power, military force, or station of birth." A world "where anyone, anywhere may express his or her beliefs, no matter how singular, without fear of being coerced into silence or conformity."

We have drifted quite far from this vision of the world. In part due to our own negligence, and in part due to the incentives and structures we've built around cyberspace primitives: closed platforms, quarterly profits, walled gardens, top-down access controls, and permissioned networks. The siren song of convenience distracted us from the more lofty ideals of liberty and prosperity. We got user acquisition but lost user freedoms in the process.

It is on us to create a new home for our minds that is worthy, welcoming, and wonderfully joyous to dwell in. We have to build the networks, the railroads, the proverbial information superhighways that allow us to assemble and communicate freely. Structures that are free and open, but also resilient, whether they are inhabited by minds made of flesh or minds made of silicon.

We have come a long way, but there's much that still needs to be built. As the doors of the legacy internet are closing—with age-verification checks, digital ID, proof-of-human, and similar measures that provide neither freedom nor safety—a new world is emerging.

OpenSats exists to fund the people who are building this world. We are grateful for the builders, and grateful for your support. We can't do it without you.

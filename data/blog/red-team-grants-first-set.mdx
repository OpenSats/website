Announcing OpenSats’ first round of grants supporting the
current red-teaming efforts going on across the entire Bitcoin
ecosystem.

In the wake of the numerous security incidents that recently hit
the Bitcoin ecosystem – such as the
[Coldcard incident][coldcard incident], the
[BTCPay Server exploit][btcpay exploit], Core Lightning
vulnerabilities, and several others – OpenSats [created][code red]
a [dedicated fund][red fund] to reimburse the cost of AI tokens
that developers and security researchers are incurring to find
and disclose vulnerabilities in critical Bitcoin infrastructure.

The developers who were awarded the first set of grants are:

- [Rob Hamilton](#rob-hamilton)
- [L0rinc](#l0rinc)
- [Knorrium](#knorrium)
- [Lrifton92](#lrifton92)
- [Kukks](#kukks)

These grants are sourced from our [Red Team Fund][red fund],
which is supported by generous donors like you. If you would
like to support red-teaming efforts for free and open-source
projects that help Bitcoin flourish, consider donating to the
fund:

### Rob Hamilton

Rob Hamilton is the CEO of [AnchorWatch][anchorwatch] – a cold
storage solution for individuals and businesses. As soon as the
consequences of the Coldcard incident were beginning to unfold,
he was [one of the first developers in the ecosystem][first red teamers],
together with a small group of open-source contributors, to start
using LLMs to scan several Bitcoin-related projects looking for
critical vulnerabilities.

Rob Hamilton and the rest of the team – which was later named
“The Red Team” – were able to find and responsibly disclose
[around 8,000 vulnerabilities][8000 vulnerabilities] in less
than a week, at the cost of tens of thousands of dollars in AI
tokens, with costs growing rapidly due to the extensive use of
those tools. They did so by leveraging the open-weight model
[Kimi K3][kimi k3] which, [unlike its US counterparts][no guardrails],
had no guardrails preventing its use in security-related tasks.

With support from this grant, Rob Hamilton will be able to
cover the cost of those AI tokens that were needed to red-team
a huge part of the Bitcoin ecosystem.

### L0rinc

[L0rinc][l0rinc] is a full-time [Bitcoin Core][bitcoin core]
developer. He received a [first grant][l0rinc first grant] from
OpenSats in December 2024, which was later
[renewed][grant renewal] in January 2025. During his years as a
contributor, l0rinc [authored around 150 pull requests][l0rinc prs]
(PRs) in Bitcoin Core and
[provided reviews to more than 400 PRs][l0rinc reviews] by other
developers. His work focused on several topics, from testing to
benchmarking, with a particular effort on optimizing the
performance of Initial Block Download (IBD) and the memory
usage across several parts of the codebase.

Since May 2026, l0rinc has been scanning both Bitcoin Core and
[libsecp256k1][libsecp256k1] using AI-assisted tools. He was
able to discover several vulnerabilities, responsibly disclosing
the issues to the maintainers and helping to fix them whenever
possible.

With support from this grant, l0rinc will be able to continue
his LLM-assisted security scanning of Bitcoin Core and
libsecp256k1, focusing on code review, [fuzzing][fuzzing], and
scrutiny of security-critical assumptions, while also expanding
his reach to other Bitcoin-related projects in case capacity
allows. Funding will help l0rinc cover costs of AI APIs and
tokens and servers to allow scanning to run continuously on the
selected projects.

### Knorrium

[Knorrium][knorrium] is a software engineer from Brazil. With
more than 20 years spent working in software development, he
gained expertise in testing automations for mobile and web
development. He has been contributing to
[mempool.space][mempool] as a quality advisor for the last five
years, focusing mainly on building end-to-end (E2E) testing and
creating a [continuous integration (CI) workflow][ci] – a way to
run automatic software building and testing after each new
development.

With support from this grant, knorrium plans to improve
[test coverage][test coverage] and build
[test vectors][test vectors] to inspect edge cases that could
lead to potentially critical vulnerabilities and exploitable code
in Bitcoin wallets and infrastructure. He is also planning to
add those testing improvements to the GitHub CI workflows.

### Lrifton92

[Lrifton92][lrifton92] is a developer and open-source contributor
who dedicated his efforts to red-teaming the Go Bitcoin
stack, such as [btcd][btcd], [lnd][lnd], [Neutrino][neutrino],
and the ecosystem running on this software stack. He leverages
[differential fuzzing][fuzzing], checking the Go codebases
against Bitcoin Core and other reference implementations for
discrepancies between them.

He has been focusing on a particular class of subtle bugs
linked to decoding and parsing paths that silently accept
malformed inputs. He was able to find several issues, such as a
missing validation that led to acceptance of out-of-range
private keys ([btcd #2545][btcd 2545]), a bug bypassing
[BIP340][bip340] requirement for a [Schnorr signature][taproot]
([btcd #2561][btcd 2561]), and a divergence from Bitcoin Core on
how btcd parses and handles trailing bytes in [PSBTs][psbt] and
in `sendrawtransaction` and `decoderawtransaction` RPC calls
(all merged by btcd maintainers in [#2558][btcd 2558]).

With support from this grant, Lrifton92 will focus on continuing
his red-teaming efforts on all those downstream projects
leveraging btcd, while also reviewing security relevant PRs for
that project. Moreover, he will expand his differential testing
suite to include validation of scripts and tapscripts and
parsing of PSBTs and MuSig2.

### Kukks

[Kukks][kukks] is CTO at [ArkLabs][arklabs] – the company behind
the [Ark][ark] implementation [Arkade][arkade] – and is one of
the most prolific contributors of [BTCPay Server][btcpayserver].
He has been contributing to the [Dotnet][dotnet] (.NET) Bitcoin
ecosystem, providing his expertise to upstream dependencies,
such as [NBitcoin][nbitcoin]. He is currently working on
building private, specialized [agent harnesses][agent harnesses]
to provide regular, fine-tuned, LLM-assisted security auditing
to the projects he is working on.

With support from this grant, Kukks will work towards expanding
his harnesses to the whole BTCPay Server stack, covering several
libraries in the .NET Bitcoin ecosystem.

---

OpenSats remains committed to fostering the security of
critical Bitcoin infrastructure. Our [Red Team Fund][red fund]
applications are fast-tracked to enable security researchers to
discover vulnerabilities in their projects before attackers do.
AI token usage can rapidly soar, and this fund helps developers
cover that cost.

We thank our donors who make these grants possible and invite
others to join us in making the Bitcoin ecosystem more
resilient. We encourage experienced developers to
[apply for funding][apply], and we welcome everyone to support
our mission.

There is a beautiful line from Mr. Rogers that is a response to what one should
do if an overwhelming amount of bad things are happening in the world. He got it
from his mother, and it goes like this:

> When I was a boy and saw scary things in the news, my mother would say, 'Look for the helpers. You will always find people who are helping.'

Look for the helpers. What a wonderful sentiment.

An overwhelming amount of things happened in Bitcoin recently. A popular
hardware wallet had a catastrophic entropy failure, BTCPay Server had a critical
bug that was actively exploited, and a chain split on top of it all, to name the
highlights of the last weeks.

Our mission as OpenSats has always been to support free and open-source projects
and contributors, especially those who make Bitcoin flourish. We want to help
those who are doing their best to make Bitcoin as useful and resilient as
possible, which is why we launched our long-term support program, and most
recently, the Red Team fund.

"You will always find people who are helping."

It was beautiful to see a widely dispersed group of people come together in the
wake of the Coldcard fallout. Dusty Daemon, one of our LTS grantees, wrote
[one of the first technical pieces] digging into the lack of software
development best practices that contributed to that failure. Rob Hamilton, who is a member of one
of our technical evaluation committees, was one of the first people to realize
the gravity of the situation, as well as the massive opportunity to do damage
reduction: "This moment may be the highest asymmetric harm reduction possible in
a generation," to quote [one of his tweets].

Calle, another one of our LTS grantees, started red teaming immediately, as did
Rob, and countless other developers. The BTCPay Server vulnerability was found
and responsibly disclosed by Craig Raw, the lead developer of Sparrow Wallet,
and another member of one of our technical committees.

"There are good people in bitcoin dropping everything out of a sense of duty to
do something," to quote Rob Hamilton once more. "ANYTHING to help strangers keep
their life savings."

Good people. Developers, builders, volunteers, fire-fighters; people who are
willing to drop everything in an instant in order to help others. People who
will do whatever is in their power to make a horrible situation a little bit
better. In other words: helpers.

We see it as our duty to allocate sats where they're needed most, as we've said
before. In other words: we want to do what we can to help the helpers, to
support those who are making sure that critical vulnerabilities are patched
before they are exploited, whether it be by malicious humans or AI systems (or
both).

If you want to help to make Bitcoin as resilient as it can be, consider making a
donation to one of our funds. If you are a developer who is working on making
bitcoin software more secure and robust, please consider applying for funding.

We are grateful for everyone who has donated so far, and we are grateful for
those who keep working on bitcoin, tirelessly, in good times and in bad.
Grateful for the helpers. Thank you.

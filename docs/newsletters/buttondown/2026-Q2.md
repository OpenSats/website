<h2 style="text-align: left;"><strong>Building the Base</strong></h2>

<p style="text-align: left;">Developing software is hard. Even with all the agentic tools that today's LLM-driven world has to offer, creating and maintaining reliable software is a difficult and strenuous task.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">Any long-lasting endeavor requires a solid foundation, and Bitcoin is no exception. OpenSats' mission is to help provide funding so that foundation can be built. From <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://opensats.org/blog/open-hardware-for-open-money"><strong><u>open hardware</u></strong></a> to <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://opensats.org/blog/the-libraries-behind-open-communication"><strong><u>nostr libraries</u></strong></a> for open and censorship-resistant communication, to <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://opensats.org/blog/call-for-applications-spring-2026"><strong><u>base-layer privacy</u></strong></a> for bitcoin itself: OpenSats funds it all.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">Whether it's a bull or bear market, we are here to support the builders. <h2 style="text-align: left;"><strong>Sats Sent</strong></h2>

<p style="text-align: left;">In Q2 2026, OpenSats sent almost 36 BTC to over 300 recipients all over the world. The transaction fees for these payouts were <code>19,435</code> sats, or $12.51 at current prices. Given that we've sent over $2.3 million this quarter, we have paid the equivalent of <strong>0.00054% in transaction fees</strong>.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">In Q2, June was our strongest month in terms of payouts, with <code>1,285,963,752</code> sats sent, followed by April and May with <code>1,207,213,206</code> sats and <code>1,086,310,722</code> sats respectively.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">As mentioned in our <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://opensats.org/newsletter/2026-Q1"><strong><u>last quarterly update</u></strong></a>, we have been sending roughly <strong>$1,000,000 worth of sats</strong> to our grantees each month. Some quarters it's a little more, some quarters it's a little less. But no matter the price, every month we sit down for our geographically distributed signing ceremony to send <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://opensats.org/topics/sats"><strong><u>sats</u></strong></a> to those who are pushing the frontier.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">We award grants on a rolling basis, meaning that our numbers will fluctuate as project-specific grants end and new grants are given. In addition to that, we run on a bitcoin standard, which means that the number of sats we have to send is fluctuating as well.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">Have a look at our <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://opensats.org/transparency"><strong><u>transparency page</u></strong></a> to dig deeper into our financial reports or browse our <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://opensats.org/projects"><strong><u>project pages</u></strong></a> to see how many sats each project has received in total.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">Bitcoin allows us to send money almost instantly, without intermediaries, without single points of failure, while being incredibly convenient and cost-efficient. It's a testament to how far we've come.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">Sign, broadcast, done.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: left;"><strong>A New Territory of Freedom</strong></h2>

<p style="text-align: left;">Permissionless protocols and the freedom tech that's built upon them open up a new territory. A territory for people to freely assemble, transact, communicate, and exchange ideas.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;"><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://satoshi.nakamotoinstitute.org/quotes/motives/"><strong><u>Satoshi Nakamoto</u></strong></a> was well aware of this when he started working on Bitcoin. He was also aware that authoritarian entities, those who love to control and silence others, will not be too happy about permissionless freedom tech. &quot;Yes,&quot; he said in agreement, conceding the point that not all political problems can be addressed through cryptographic means, &quot;but we can win a major battle in the arms race and gain a new territory of freedom for several years.&quot;</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">As permissionless technology proliferates, this new territory of freedom will continue to be attacked and subverted, by legal and other means. The most recent example is a takedown request by the Indian government sent to our LTS grantee Calle, regarding the aforementioned bitchat app.</p>

<figure><img src="https://opensats.org/static/images/newsletter/2026-Q2/bitchat-takedown-request.jpg" alt="Email from GitHub Trust &amp; Safety relaying a takedown notice from the Indian government for the bitchat repositories" width="640" style="max-width:100%;height:auto;" draggable="false"><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

<p style="text-align: left;">Allow me to <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://satoshi.nakamotoinstitute.org/quotes/double-spending/#:~:text=It%20takes%20advantage%20of%20the%20nature%20of%20information%20being%20easy%20to%20spread%20but%20hard%20to%20stifle."><strong><u>quote Satoshi</u></strong></a> once more, before we get into Calle's response to the government's takedown request: &quot;It takes advantage of the nature of information being easy to spread but hard to stifle.&quot;</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">Easy to spread, hard to stifle. Such is the nature of information.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">The bitchat source code is now mirrored across a multitude of censorship-resistant protocols, including the nostr-native <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://opensats.org/projects/ngit"><strong><u>ngit</u></strong></a> funded by OpenSats. And in addition to all of the above, <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://njump.to/nevent1qvzqqqqqqypzq5xeflpdskqvdq4swxj59793uvdzqzc9pzatjk3nhmcg2h0js8trqyt8wumn8ghj76rfwd6zumn0wd68ytnvv9hxgtcpzpmhxue69uhkummnw3ezumt0d5hsqg8k4snmlexqmryf903g0jnm0r83kv36us4sumc7n3spsy8apc3dyvppr4zs"><strong><u>bitchat can now send itself to another phone</u></strong></a> with no internet required.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">&quot;We know that,&quot; as <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/cypherpunk-manifesto/"><strong><u>Eric Hughes</u></strong></a> wrote in 1993, &quot;software can’t be destroyed and that a widely dispersed system can’t be shut down.&quot;</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">The new territory of freedom that the early cypherpunks describe is made possible by two fundamental truths: 1) code is speech, speech is information, and information is notoriously hard to stifle. 2) Strong cryptography empowers individuals and disempowers oppressors, inverting hierarchies that have stood for millennia. As long as these truths hold, building a home in this new territory will be a possibility.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: left;"><strong>A New Home of Mind</strong></h2>

<p style="text-align: left;">As we're heading further into the agentic age, the age of machine intelligence, I can't help but think of the opening line of John Perry Barlow's <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.eff.org/cyberspace-independence"><strong><u><em>A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace</em></u></strong></a>, written in 1996:</p>

<blockquote><p style="text-align: left;"><strong><em>Governments of the Industrial World, you weary giants of flesh and steel, I come from Cyberspace, the new home of Mind.</em></strong></p></blockquote>

<p style="text-align: left;">Barlow wrote that &quot;cyberspace consists of transactions, relationships, and thought itself,&quot; a construct that is created and made possible by the very act of communication between peers. He describes &quot;a world that all may enter without privilege or prejudice accorded by race, economic power, military force, or station of birth.&quot; A world &quot;where anyone, anywhere may express his or her beliefs, no matter how singular, without fear of being coerced into silence or conformity.&quot;</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">We have drifted quite far from this vision of the world. In part due to our own negligence, and in part due to the incentives and structures we've built around cyberspace primitives: closed platforms, quarterly profits, walled gardens, top-down access controls, and permissioned networks. The siren song of convenience distracted us from the more lofty ideals of liberty and prosperity. We got user acquisition but lost user freedoms in the process.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">It is on us to create a new home for our minds that is worthy, welcoming, and wonderfully joyous to dwell in. We have to build the networks, the railroads, the proverbial information superhighways that allow us to assemble and communicate freely. Structures that are free and open, but also resilient, whether they are inhabited by minds made of flesh or minds made of silicon.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">We have come a long way, but there's much that still needs to be built. As the doors of the legacy internet are closing—with age-verification checks, digital ID, proof-of-human, and similar measures that provide neither freedom nor safety—a new world is emerging. A new territory of freedom. A new home of mind.</p>

<p style="text-align: left;">OpenSats exists to fund the people who are claiming and creating this territory, who are building this home and world.</p>
